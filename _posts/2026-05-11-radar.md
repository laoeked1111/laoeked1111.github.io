For our 6.2300 EM Waves & Applications class [project](https://github.com/joshop/62300-horn-antenna-radar), Ian Frankel, Joshua Piety, and I created a bistatic radar system for distance measurement.

## System Overview

A [bistatic radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bistatic_radar) system is a radar system with separate transmit and receive antennas. By measuring the time of flight of a transmitted radar pulse to return, an estimate for the distance between the radar and the target can be obtained by multiplying by the speed of light in the medium (air). For convenient and precise measurement of the time of flight, we used our course-provided NanoVNA v2, with the TX and RX antennas connected to port 1 and 2 respectively.

![bistatic system](/img/portfolio/3d-printed-radar/bistatic-radar.png)

## Antenna Design

Using parametric design in Onshape, we CADed conical and exponential horn antenna profiles optimized for operation at 2.45 GHz. A half-wavelength feed at the back of the antenna is used to transmit and receive the EM waves. 

![cad](/img/portfolio/3d-printed-radar/cad.png)

We then took this design and simulated its directionality and performance in Ansys HFSS. A plot of the simulated return loss for the conical design is shown below. We believe part of the reason why the maximal dip is not exactly at 2.45 GHz is due to a static feed antenna length which was somewhat difficult to optimize in simulation.

![hfss](/img/portfolio/3d-printed-radar/antenna_animation_gif.gif)

![sim return loss](/img/portfolio/3d-printed-radar/simulated-return-loss.png)

Motivated by these simulations, we 3D-printed the antennas in PLA and covered the inside with conductive tape. We then attached an SMA cable with the feed antenna inside and incrementally trimmed the antenna length until the measured return loss of the antenna was satisfactorily low at the operating frequency.

![measured return loss](/img/portfolio/3d-printed-radar/measured-return-loss.png)

## GUI and Results

We implemented a Python-based GUI using Imgui and Scikit-RF to communicate between a laptop and the NanoVNA v2 over USB. The GUI enables SOLT calibration, S11 and S21 measurements, and a waterfall diagram visualizing the distance measurement from the radar system. Since the NanoVNA v2 was measuring received radar pulses in the frequency domain, we took the IFFT of the received signal and applied background subtraction to arrive at the data for display.

![gui](/img/portfolio/3d-printed-radar/gui.png)

While testing the radar, we deduced that there was a constant ~30cm offset between measured and real distance due to the physical size of the antennas, so we corrected for it and found reasonably high accuracy in our radar system.
